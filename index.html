INCIDENT REPORT · 00:17 HOURS

Someone hijacked the midnight frequency.

At 00:17, independent radio station WNDR-93.7 broadcast a message that was never scheduled. The transmission lasted 83 seconds. Then the station went silent. The transmitter room was locked from the inside, the backup generator never tripped, and the studio log has been edited.

The five people with access

Mara Voss · Producer

Controls the broadcast schedule and keeps the physical studio keys.

Ivo Renn · Engineer

Maintains the transmitter and the emergency relay network.

Sana Quill · Archivist

Catalogues tapes and stores the station's original transmission logs.

Theo Vale · Night host

Reads the overnight bulletins and signs off the live microphone.

June Park · Intern

Runs errands between booths and prepares the overnight ledger.

Case rule: Do not assume the person who spoke on air was the person who initiated the transmission. The relay system can carry a pre-recorded signal.

Your objective

  1. Reconstruct the route taken by the signal through the relay network.
  2. Decode the visual signal grid.
  3. Rebuild the night roster from the clues.
  4. Decrypt the recovered tape.
  5. Use all four results to identify who had the access, opportunity and motive.

PUZZLE 02 · NONOGRAM / PIXEL LOGIC

Dead pixels, live signal

The station's waveform monitor saved a 15×15 monochrome image before the broadcast cut out. Each numbered clue describes a run of consecutive dark pixels in that row or column.

How to solve this grid

  1. Read the numbers at the left of a row from left to right. Each number is the length of one uninterrupted run of dark squares.
  2. Read the numbers above a column from top to bottom. They describe the dark runs in that column.
  3. There must be at least one pale square between two runs. For example, clue 3 2 means three dark squares, at least one pale square, then two dark squares.
  4. Click a square once to mark it dark, twice to mark it pale, and a third time to clear it. Use both dark and pale marks. Unmarked squares are not counted as solved.
  5. When every square is marked and all clues match, select Check image. The revealed image is the extraction answer.
Example: A row clue of 4 1 means a block of four dark squares, one or more pale squares, then one dark square. The block may slide left or right as long as the entire row and column clues remain possible.

PUZZLE 04 · POLYALPHABETIC CRYPTANALYSIS

The tape marked LANTERN

A reel-to-reel tape was found behind the transmitter rack. Its label reads LANTERN. The message is encrypted using the Vigenère cipher, a repeating-key cipher.

Recovered ciphertext

EHR MVRADMVMXVE TS HGHVE EHR GSIGS SGTMI

Decoding instructions

  1. Write the key LANTERN repeatedly beneath the letters of the ciphertext. Ignore spaces when repeating the key.
  2. Convert letters to numbers: A=0, B=1, … Z=25.
  3. For each ciphertext letter, subtract the matching key letter value. If the result is negative, add 26.
  4. Convert the result back to a letter. Keep spaces in their original positions.
Formula: plaintext = (ciphertext − key) mod 26. Example: ciphertext D (3) minus key B (1) gives C (2).

The key is not a clue to the answer. It is the repeated sequence used to shift the ciphertext letters.

Decode the first three letters separately: E−L wraps around the alphabet to T; H−A is H; R−N is E.

PUZZLE 05 · FINAL INQUIRY

Who put the station on air?

The decoded tape gives a location. The route gives the path the signal travelled. The roster tells you who occupied the relevant booth. Now test the explanation against the surviving evidence.

Transmission log

The emergency relay was activated from the terminal at the end of the shortest legal route. No live microphone was opened.

Access register

The Booth A key was signed out by the final operator. The key was returned at 00:21, four minutes after the broadcast ended.

Archive note

The original tape was catalogued by Sana. Its label was changed from “North stair” to “LANTERN” after midnight.

Studio microphone

Theo's microphone remained physically muted throughout the 83-second transmission.

Case resolution

The decoded tape says the transmitter is under the north stair. The route map establishes the relay path; the roster shows Ivo was the final operator and had Booth A. The access register places the Booth A key with the final operator during the broadcast window. The transmitter engineer had the technical access needed to activate the emergency relay. Ivo is the strongest supported culprit. The tape's changed label and Sana's archive role explain how the instruction was concealed, but do not prove she initiated the transmission.
